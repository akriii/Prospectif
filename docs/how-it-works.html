3. District fundamentals (seed.py)

For each of the 12 districts the seeder stores:

FundamentalSourceDerivation
PopulationOpenDOSM populationPerak, sex = both, age = overall, ethnicity = overall, latest date; × 1,000 (CSV is in thousands).
Employed workforceOpenDOSM labour forcelf_employed, latest date, × 1,000. Stored in a column named new_jobs_created, but it is total employed persons.
Median household incomeOpenDOSM incomeincome_median from the latest survey.
Unsold housing unitsNAPICUnsold residential units, summed per district for 2026.

District names differ between sources, so they are reconciled: DOSM's "Larut Dan Matang" and "Selama" are merged into "Larut, Matang & Selama" (population and jobs summed, income averaged).

One fundamental is computed live in predict.py:

cafes & malls per 10k residents = lifestyle anchors in district ÷ population × 10,000
Kinta:  1,897 ÷ 931,000 × 10,000 = 20.4

4. How the district score is calculated

Step 1 — Normalise every metric to 0–1 (min-max)

normalised = (value − lowest district) ÷ (highest district − lowest district)

The best district becomes 1.0, the worst 0.0. Example — income: lowest is Hulu Perak (RM 3,701), highest is Manjung (RM 5,380):

Kinta:  (5,136 − 3,701) ÷ (5,380 − 3,701) = 0.855
MetricMeaning
norm_jobsEmployed workforce (Kinta 448,500 = 1.0; Hulu Perak 30,700 = 0.0)
norm_incomeMedian household income
norm_overhangUnsold housing; 1.0 = the most unsold (Kinta, 1,420 units)
norm_lifestyleCafes & malls per 10k residents
edu_indexProxy, not real data: 1.0 for Kampar, Muallim, Perak Tengah, Kinta (university towns); 0.4 for all others

Step 2 — Apply the pillar's weighted formula (services/layers.py)

"Inverted" terms use 1 − value, meaning lower is better (e.g. less unsold housing is good).

PillarFormulaAnchors used
Apex Score30% jobs + 30% income + 20% edu + 20% (1 − overhang)all three
Talent Magnet60% edu + 40% (1 − income)education, lifestyle
Retail Vacuum40% income + 30% jobs + 30% (1 − lifestyle density)industry
Housing Gap40% jobs + 30% income + 30% (1 − overhang)all three

Step 3 — District ranking

Ranking districts is simply sorting their scores. For Apex: Kinta 0.756 → Muallim 0.606 → Perak Tengah 0.576 → Manjung 0.534 → Kampar 0.525 → … → Hulu Perak 0.275. The map does not show this list directly; it shows hexagon cells built on top of it.

5. How each point learns its district (PostGIS)

An OSM cafe only has a latitude and longitude. resolve_districts() sends every point to the database and asks "which district polygon contains this point?":

JOIN districts d ON ST_Covers(d.geom, ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint(lon, lat), 4326))

The GiST spatial index makes this fast, and the answer is cached in memory, so it runs once per server start. Each point then inherits its district's score as its weight:

{ "name": "Starbucks", "lat": 4.595, "lon": 101.089,
  "category": "lifestyle", "district": "Kinta", "weight": 0.756 }

Every point in Kinta therefore carries the same weight, 0.756. The API returns ~4,375 such points together with the formula and the full district breakdown.

6. Hexagons, anchors & cell index (lib/hexbin.js)

What are anchors?

Anchors are real places from OpenStreetMap — evidence that activity exists at a location:

  • Lifestyle — cafes and malls
  • Education — universities and colleges
  • Industry — industrial zones

Each pillar uses specific anchor types; Retail Vacuum, for example, uses only industrial zones (where the workers are).

How the hexagons are built

  1. Perak is covered with an invisible honeycomb of hexagons, 1.5 km from centre to corner.
  2. Each point is converted from latitude/longitude to metres (a local flat projection, accurate to under 1% at Perak's latitude) and dropped into the hexagon it falls in, using axial hex-grid coordinates with cube rounding.
  3. Only hexagons containing at least one anchor exist — empty jungle shows nothing. For Apex that gives 272 cells.

What is the cell index?

cell index = sum of the weights of every anchor inside the hexagon
           = (number of anchors) × (district score)     ← when all fall in one district

Downtown Ipoh cell:  527 anchors × 0.756 = 398.41
Hulu Perak cell:       2 anchors × 0.275 =   0.55
The cell index rewards both a strong district and a high concentration of real activity. That combination is what the dashboard calls a micro-city.

How cells are ranked and coloured

Raw cell indexes span 0.3 to 398, so colouring them directly would paint only Ipoh red and everything else identical blue. Instead each cell gets a percentile rank — the share of all cells it beats (ties share a mid-rank). The percentile is split into seven colour steps:

0–14% · lowPerak median86–100% · high
PercentileVerdict label
≥ 85%Prime opportunity
≥ 60%Strong opportunity
≥ 40%Moderate opportunity
< 40%Weak opportunity
"Which micro-city is best?" — the deep-red hexagons: the top ~14% of all 1.5 km cells in Perak for the selected pillar.

7. How the map is drawn (MapView.jsx)

The map is a stack of transparent layers rendered on the GPU by deck.gl, bottom to top:

#LayerWhat you see
1Basemap (MapLibre + CARTO)Roads, towns, sea. Positron in light mode, Dark Matter in dark mode. A background picture only — it knows nothing about the data.
2District boundaries (GeoJsonLayer)The 12 outlines, served from PostGIS and simplified with ST_SimplifyPreserveTopology (~100 m tolerance) down to 73 KB.
3District names (TextLayer)Placed at the centre of each district's main polygon; hidden past zoom 10.5.
4Glow heatmap (toggle)Blurred density cloud of the same points, for presentations.
5Hexagons (PolygonLayer)The coloured cells; hoverable and clickable. In glow mode they become invisible but stay clickable.
6Selected hexagonThick outline around the clicked cell.
7Anchor pins (ScatterplotLayer)Real places, coloured by category.

Zoom handover

Between zoom 11 and 12.5 the hexagons fade to 15% strength while the anchor pins fade in. Zoomed out, the map answers "where is the opportunity?"; zoomed in, it answers "these are the actual cafes, colleges and factories that prove it".

8. Tooltip, drawer & verdict

Hover a hexagon

Shows the district, rank ("Top 1%"), cell index, district score, anchor counts and the formula's metric values.

Click a hexagon — the analytics drawer

  • Verdict — rule-based, not AI: rank band + strongest factor + weakest factor + a pillar-specific recommendation. Example: "Its strongest driver is employment base (1.00); the main drag is low unsold housing overhang (0.00). Prioritise for flagship mixed-use or township development."
  • Score breakdown — one bar per formula term. The grey track is the term's weight; the purple fill is how much of it the district earns.
  • Anchors in this cell — count per category and up to six named places.
  • District fundamentals — population, workforce, income, unsold units, cafes per 10k.
  • Zoom to anchors — flies the camera in so the pins appear.

9. Known limitations

  1. One score per district. All places in a district share its score. Cells in Ipoh beat cells in Batu Gajah only because Ipoh has more anchors, not better local economics. Finer differences need sub-district (mukim) data.
  2. Cafe density dominates. 4,007 lifestyle anchors versus 105 education and 263 industry: for Apex and Housing, "red" largely means "many cafes in a good district".
  3. edu_index is a placeholder (hardcoded 1.0 / 0.4), not measured data.
  4. "Jobs" means total employed persons, not new jobs created, despite the column name.
  5. Rankings are relative. "Top 1%" means best within Perak for that pillar, not good in absolute terms.
